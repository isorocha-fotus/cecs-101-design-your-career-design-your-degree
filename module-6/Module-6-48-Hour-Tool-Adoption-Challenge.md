# 48-Hour Tool Adoption Challenge
### Building Adaptability in the Age of AI

Technology doesn't wait for you to feel ready. New tools — especially AI-powered ones — show up constantly, and the people who thrive are the ones who can learn them quickly instead of avoiding them. This assignment puts you in that exact situation on purpose: you'll pick an unfamiliar tool, give yourself only 48 hours to get functional with it, and pay close attention to *how* you learn, not just *what* you learn.

---

## Learning Objective

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:
- Learn a new, unfamiliar tool under a real time constraint
- Identify the specific strategies that help you adapt quickly
- Reflect honestly on the discomfort of not knowing something yet, and how you moved through it

---

## Step 1 — Choose Your Tool (Do this first, before the clock starts)

Select a tool or app you have **never used before**. It should be:

- **New to you** — not something you've dabbled in before
- **Ideally AI-related** (an AI writing tool, image generator, AI-powered productivity app, coding assistant, transcription tool, etc.) — though a non-AI tool is acceptable if you check with your instructor first
- **Free or accessible** without a paid subscription
- **Realistic in scope** — something you could plausibly learn the basics of in two days, not a professional-grade software suite that takes months to master

**Need ideas?** Ask your instructor for a list of suggested tools, or propose your own for approval before starting the clock.

## Step 3 — Learn the Tool (Over the Next 48 Hours)

Dive in. Explore the tool, watch tutorials if needed, experiment, make mistakes, and figure it out. There's no single "right" way to do this — the point is to notice your *own* process as you go.

### Journal Requirement

Keep a short journal during the 48 hours. You don't need to write an essay each time — a few honest sentences per entry is enough. Make **at least 3 journal entries** across the two days (for example: one shortly after you start, one partway through, and one near the end).

For each entry, note:
- **What you tried** (what did you do in this session?)
- **What was confusing or frustrating** (where did you get stuck? What made you want to give up?)
- **What helped** (a tutorial, a friend, trial and error, reading the FAQ, something else?)
- **How you felt** (be honest — overwhelmed, curious, bored, excited, embarrassed, etc.)

**Journal Entry 1** — *(shortly after starting)*
- What I tried: I started by asking Gemini for playful cursive fonts, then for fonts that look more professional and elegant and feel like growth. I asked for examples and a list with images, picked Aether Blow, and asked it to write "Honeybee" in that font. Then I had it make the word gold with a honeycomb background behind it.
- What was confusing or frustrating: Gemini told me it couldn't render the exact font I named, so it generated its own version of "Honeybee" instead. There were also so many categories of script fonts that it was hard to tell which one actually looked elegant and not just fancy.
- What helped: Asking for pictures instead of descriptions. Seeing examples side by side made it much easier to know what I liked, and narrowing my request from "playful" to "professional and elegant" got me closer fast.
- How I felt: Curious and excited. This part felt like brainstorming, and the first gold "Honeybee" already looked better than I expected.

**Journal Entry 2** — *(partway through)*
- What I tried: I kept editing in Gemini. I made the gold match the color I wanted, turned the honeycomb into a logo-style bee, and added a stinger. Then I tried a new version where the last "e" flows into a doodle of a bee, and asked it over and over to make that bee match the first one, with the same view from above showing its back and wings. Eventually I had it isolate the bee by itself and make a clean version of just the word with no background.
- What was confusing or frustrating: Every small change seemed to change something else. When I said "don't change a thing, just add a stinger," it sometimes still redrew parts of the image. It kept attaching the bee to the "y" in "Honey" instead of the last "e," even after I told it multiple times.
- What helped: Splitting the logo into separate pieces. Once I had the isolated bee and the isolated word as two clean images, I stopped asking one tool to do everything at once. I asked Gemini how to get ChatGPT to put them together, and it wrote me a detailed prompt.
- How I felt: Frustrated, especially with the bee landing on the "y" again and again. But I could tell I was getting closer each time, which kept me going.

**Journal Entry 3** — *(near the end)*
- What I tried: I uploaded the isolated word and bee to ChatGPT with the prompts Gemini wrote for me. When the bee came out too big, tilted straight up, or with a melted, distorted head, I went back to Gemini for a better prompt and started a brand-new ChatGPT chat each time so it wouldn't carry over old mistakes. In my final chat, I swapped in my reference bee to keep it symmetrical, lined the stinger up with the "e," expanded the canvas, and had it cut the background so only the logo was left.
- What was confusing or frustrating: Telling it to make the bee "smaller" barely did anything. I got a lot of "so close" results that were never quite right, and I deleted and restarted several chats.
- What helped: Using exact numbers instead of vague words, like making the bee no larger than a lowercase "e" and angling it at 45 degrees. The biggest help was something I noticed myself: the image was cramped, so the AI kept squeezing the bee and making it bigger to fit. Asking for a wider canvas with extra empty space on the right finally fixed the size and angle.
- How I felt: Worn out during the restarts, then relieved and proud once the final logo looked like what I had in my head.

## Step 5 — Final Reflection (Write this after your 48 hours are up)

Write a reflection of **8–12 sentences** responding to the following:

- What made this tool difficult to learn at first? Be specific.
- Which strategy helped you adapt the *fastest* — and why do you think it worked?
- Did your feelings about the challenge change from the beginning to the end? How?
- What does this experience tell you about how you personally learn new things under pressure?
- How might this same approach help you the next time a new tool, app, or AI system shows up unexpectedly at school or work?

**My Reflection:**
What made this tool difficult at first was that I didn't understand how an image generator follows directions. I'd ask for one small change, like adding a stinger or moving the bee, and it would redraw half the image, stick the bee on the "y," or melt the bee's head into a lump. Words like "smaller" barely did anything, so I kept getting results that were close but never right. The strategy that helped me adapt fastest was splitting the work between two tools: I used Gemini to break the logo into separate pieces and write precise prompts, then gave those prompts to ChatGPT in a fresh chat. That worked because a fresh chat cleared out the mistakes the model kept carrying over, and exact instructions like "no larger than a lowercase e" and "45 degrees" gave it something concrete to aim for. The biggest breakthrough was my own, though: I noticed the image was cramped, so the AI kept squeezing the bee and making it bigger to fit, and asking for a wider canvas fixed it. At the start I was excited and just exploring fonts, but by the middle I was frustrated, especially when the bee kept landing in the same wrong spot. By the end, that frustration turned into satisfaction, because I had figured out how to talk to the tool, not just what to ask it for. This tells me I learn best under pressure by doing, failing fast, and adjusting, not by reading instructions first. It also showed me that starting over can be faster than forcing something that's stuck. The next time a new tool or AI system shows up at school or work, I'll break the task into small pieces, use exact numbers instead of vague words, use a second tool to help me ask better questions, and start fresh when I get stuck.
