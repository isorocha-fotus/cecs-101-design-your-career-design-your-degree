# Module 5: Contingency Planning — Plan A, Plan B, and Plan C

This activity develops three distinct but connected career-degree scenarios. Each one is grounded in my values and strengths, points toward a direction I would actually want, and prepares me for a different version of my professional future.

---

## Step 1 — Review My Foundation

Before building these plans, I re-read my anchors:

- **[POV Statement](../module-3/Module-3-POV-Statement-Activity.md) (Module 3):** *I am a senior who started in food science, switched into Applied AI, and is building my own food and beverage brand and client business while finishing my degree. I need a path that lets me combine technology, science, and business so I can build products that people love, because I've learned that I'm most motivated and most capable when I take an idea all the way from the lab or the laptop to a real customer.*
- **[Career Vision Statement](../module-4/Module-4-Career-Vision-Statement-Activity.md) (Module 4):** Build technology and food products with small food and beverage producers, as a founder or technical leader, so that great products made by small teams can reach customers without needing a big company's resources.
- **[RIASEC Profile](../module-4/Module-4-RIASEC-Assessment-and-Build-Your-Profile.md) (Module 4):** **E-I-C.** Enterprising is by far my strongest type, Investigative is second, and Conventional tied with Social for third. I want to lead and build with technical knowledge, not only be the technical person.

All three plans below come from the same core: building real products where food and technology meet, and getting them in front of real customers. What changes is *where* I do that work and *which* of my skills leads.

## Step 3 — Comparative Reflection

**1. What do all three plans have in common? What does that tell me about my core direction?**
**2. Which plan feels most authentic to who I am right now? Which one challenges me the most?**
**3. If I woke up tomorrow and had to commit to Plan B immediately, what would be the first three steps I would take?**

**My Reflection:**

All three plans have me building or improving products that real people buy, at the point where food and technology meet. That tells me my core direction isn't a job title. It's taking an idea all the way to a customer, which is exactly what my POV Statement says. The only thing that changes between the plans is whether I do that work in my own company, in someone else's sales team, or in a food lab.

Plan A feels most authentic to who I am right now, because I'm already doing a small version of it with my brand and my client work, and it fits my Enterprising side best. Plan C challenges me the most. It would mean more time in the lab, less ownership, possibly more school, and competing with people who have full Food Science degrees. Writing it out made me realize that if I want Plan C to stay a real option, I can't drop the Food Science minor casually.

If I had to commit to Plan B tomorrow, my first step would be making a list of 15 food-tech and software companies that sell to food producers and checking their open solutions and sales engineer roles. Second, I'd message two working sales engineers on LinkedIn and ask for 20-minute informational interviews. Third, I'd rewrite my resume and portfolio around my client work, showing each project as a customer problem I listened to, scoped, demoed, and delivered.
