# Module 5: Stress-Testing My Career-Degree Plan

This activity identifies the weak spots, assumptions, and single points of failure in my [Integrated Career-Degree Roadmap](Module-5-Integrated-Career-Degree-Roadmap.md), and the strategies I'll use to address them.

---

## Step 1 — The Five Stress Tests

### Stress Test 1: Interest Drift

**My Scenario:** After taking AI 301 and AI 302 in Spring 2027, I find that I don't enjoy hands-on coding as much as I expected, and I'm pulled more toward the business side or toward running my own brand full time. I already changed direction once, switching from food science to Applied AI, so I know my interests can shift.

**Impact on My Roadmap:** My remaining major courses (AI 401, CECS 499) and my career path as an AI developer would be affected the most. Changing majors again as a senior isn't realistic, so the risk is finishing a degree that leads to a career I don't want.

**Vulnerability Rating:** 2 / 5

**Mitigation Strategy:** My plan already spreads across technology, food science, and business, so a shift in interest would change which career I pick, not whether my degree is useful. To catch drift early, I'll use my internship and informational interviews to test what I actually enjoy day to day. I'll keep my Career Exploration Map options open (AI developer, sales engineer, food product developer, founder) instead of locking into one job title.

### Stress Test 2: Market Disruption

**My Scenario:** AI tools get good enough that companies hire far fewer entry-level developers, which is the type of job I'd be applying for. AI also makes building software so easy that many people start offering the same kind of costing and formulation tools I plan to build for small food producers.

**Impact on My Roadmap:** This hits my Stage 4 job search and my career vision. If entry-level developer jobs shrink, I'd face more competition for fewer roles. If anyone can build the tools, my business idea would have to stand out some other way. On top of that, I haven't confirmed that small producers want those tools at all.

**Vulnerability Rating:** 3 / 5

**Mitigation Strategy:** My Food Science minor is my protection. Fewer developers understand both the software and the food science behind a product, and that combination is harder to automate. I'll lean into the people-facing skills AI can't replace easily (working with clients, explaining technical ideas, selling), and I'll interview 10 small food and beverage producers by March 2027 to test whether my tool idea solves a real problem before I build my career around it.

### Stress Test 3: Academic Obstacles

**My Scenario:** My Spring 2027 semester is overloaded: AI 202, AI 301, and AI 302, plus CHEM 260 and MICR 210 as prerequisites for my Food Science minor. If I struggle in one course, or a required AI course is only offered once a year and doesn't fit my schedule, I could have to retake it or push it back.

**Impact on My Roadmap:** My Fall 2027 graduation date is the most affected. AI 401 and CECS 499 depend on finishing my earlier major courses, and FDSC 410 and FDSC 421 depend on CHEM 260 and MICR 210. One failed or delayed course could delay graduation by a full semester.

**Vulnerability Rating:** 4 / 5

**Mitigation Strategy:** I'll meet with my advisor before Spring 2027 registration to confirm that all three AI courses will be offered and to check my remaining TOPS hours. I'm also deciding now that the Food Science minor is optional. If CHEM 260 or MICR 210 puts my graduation at risk, I'll drop the minor rather than delay my degree, and I'll keep learning food science through my own brand instead.

### Stress Test 4: Life Interruptions

**My Scenario:** I'm carrying a full course load, client work, and my own confectionery and beverage brand, which I fund myself. Burnout, money pressure, or a health issue could force me to cut back on classes or on my business.

**Impact on My Roadmap:** If I had to reduce my course load, Spring 2027 and Fall 2027 would be affected and graduation could move to Spring 2028. If I had to cut back on work, I'd lose client projects and income, which are part of my SMART goals and my portfolio.

**Vulnerability Rating:** 3 / 5

**Mitigation Strategy:** I'll protect one full day off each week (SMART Goal 5) and cap my client work at 3 active clients during the semester so school stays first. I'll hold off on major spending for my brand until after graduation. If I have to drop a course, I'll talk to my advisor right away about a realistic Spring 2028 graduation plan instead of trying to push through everything.

### Stress Test 5: Competitive Reality

**My Scenario:** I apply to internships for Summer 2027 and don't get any offers. AI and tech internships get hundreds of applicants, and my experience is mostly self-directed client work rather than a past internship.

**Impact on My Roadmap:** This is a single point of failure in my plan. The internship requirement (CECS 375) is required for my degree, and my Stage 3 transition from student to professional depends on it. If I don't land one, the requirement moves to Fall 2027 and makes my final semester much heavier.

**Vulnerability Rating:** 4 / 5

**Mitigation Strategy:** I'll apply to at least 10 internships by January 31, 2027 (SMART Goal 3) and widen my search to food and beverage companies, where my mix of AI and food science stands out more than at pure tech companies. My degree lets me meet the requirement through CECS 375, 385, 475, or 485, so I'll ask my advisor which of those could be my backup if I don't get an internship. I'll also ask whether my client work could count toward the requirement.

---

## Step 2 — My Three Biggest Vulnerabilities

1. **Most Critical: Competitive Reality (not landing an internship).** The internship requirement is required for my degree, and my whole move from student to professional is built around a Summer 2027 internship I haven't secured yet. If it falls through, my last semester gets overloaded and my graduation timeline is at risk.

2. **Second Most Critical: Academic Obstacles (my Spring 2027 course load).** Five demanding courses in one semester leaves no room for error, and the courses in my final semester depend on finishing them. The risk is real, but I control more of it, because I can drop the minor if I need to.

3. **Third Most Critical: Market Disruption (AI and an untested business idea).** AI could shrink the entry-level developer jobs I'm targeting, and my career vision rests on an assumption I haven't tested: that small food producers want and would pay for my tools. This is less urgent than the first two, but it affects my long-term direction the most.

---

## Step 3 — Stress-Test Reflection

Before this stress-test, I assumed that my plan was solid because every course was mapped out semester by semester and I'd already finished my general education requirements. After examining my plan carefully, I now realize that the biggest risks aren't in my coursework but in the things I don't control: whether I land an internship, whether my Spring 2027 load is realistic, and whether small food producers actually want the tools I plan to build. Several parts of my plan depend on a single outcome going right, with no backup. The most important change I need to make to my roadmap is building a real Plan B for the internship requirement, by talking to my advisor about the CECS 385, 475, and 485 options and applying early and widely. I'm also deciding ahead of time that I'd drop the Food Science minor before delaying graduation. Running this analysis has changed the way I think about planning, because I now see a good plan as one that survives when things go wrong, not one where everything has to go right.
