## Roadmap Narrative

The clearest thread running through all four stages is building real products at the point where food and technology meet. My first two years were all about food: how ingredients behave, how products are preserved, and how confections are made. My third year added business and AI, and my senior year puts all of it to work through client projects, an internship, and a capstone. Even my switch into Applied AI wasn't a change in direction. It added the technology I needed to build tools around the food products I already cared about.

The lane that feels most underdeveloped is Network and Visibility. For most of college I worked on my own, building my brand without deliberately meeting people in my target industries. To fix that, I'm going to join the IFT Student Association, request informational interviews with professionals like Mickey Miller at Bush Brothers, attend career fairs this spring, and post my finished work on LinkedIn instead of keeping it to myself.

My transition from student to professional starts in Stage 3, specifically in Summer 2027, when I complete my CECS 375 internship while running my own client work. That moment is significant because it's the first time I'll do this work inside a real company, with a supervisor and teammates who depend on me, instead of only for myself. It's also when I plan to test the assumption my career vision rests on by interviewing small food producers about whether they actually want the tools I hope to build.

This roadmap tells me I'm becoming a builder who can work on both sides of a product: the science of making it and the technology and business of getting it to people. My path hasn't been a straight line, but every stage added something I'll use. By the time I graduate, I want to be someone who doesn't just have ideas but has already tested them with real customers, and who can decide with confidence whether to grow my own company or bring those skills to someone else's.
