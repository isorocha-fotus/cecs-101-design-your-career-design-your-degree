# Career Vision Statement Activity

This activity guides you through a structured writing process to develop a meaningful Career Vision Statement. You will build it in layers — starting with raw material from your previous work, developing a draft, refining it, and then committing to a final version.

---

## Step 1 — Gather Your Raw Material

Before writing, pull together the following from your previous work:

- Your Personal Empathy Map insights
- Your SWOT Analysis
- Your Personal POV Statement
- Your RIASEC Profile
- Your Career Exploration Map

**What I pulled from each:**
- **Empathy Map:** I'm happiest when I'm building something and putting it in front of real people, and I take on too much.
- **SWOT:** My biggest strength is that I can both make a product and build the technology around it. The biggest opportunity is a gap in tools for small food producers.
- **POV Statement:** I need a path that combines technology, science, and business so I can build products people love.
- **RIASEC Profile:** E-I-C. Enterprising is by far my strongest type, with Investigative second.
- **Career Exploration Map:** No single career matched all of me. AI developer fit my degree, sales engineer fit my strongest interest, and food product developer fit my passion. The people I researched showed that combining fields is normal.

---

## Step 2 — Answer the Foundation Questions

**What kind of work do I want to do?**
*(Think about tasks, not job titles. What activities do you want to fill your professional days with? What problems do you want to work on?)*
I want my days filled with building and testing: designing a tool or a product, trying it with real users, seeing what breaks, and improving it. I want to work on problems where technology and food meet, like figuring out what a product costs to make, tuning a formula, and scaling a recipe from a small batch to something a customer can buy. I also want to be the person who explains the idea and sells it, not only the person who builds it.

**Who do I want to serve or impact?**
*(Who are the people, communities, or sectors your work will benefit? Be as specific as you can.)*
Small food and beverage producers and the small businesses around them. These are people making a good product who don't have access to the tools big companies use for costing, formulation, and scale-up. I know what that gap feels like because I'm working through it with my own confectionery and beverage brand and my client projects.

**What values do I want my career to embody?**
Independence and ownership, so that I'm responsible for what I make. Practical creativity, meaning ideas that actually get built and used. Honesty and quality, because food and software both break trust if they aren't done right. And continuous learning, because both AI and food science keep changing.

**What kind of professional do I want to become?**
*(What qualities, character traits, or reputation do you want to be known for in your field?)*
Someone who finishes what they start and can explain complicated things clearly to people who aren't technical. I want to be known as the person who understands both the science and the technology, reliable with clients, and honest about what a tool or a product can and can't do.

**What does success look like for me — not financially, but meaningfully?**
*(Set salary aside for a moment. What would make you feel that your career was truly worthwhile?)*
Success would be a small producer using something I built to get a better product on a shelf, and being able to point to real people who are doing better work because of it. It would also mean owning my own company or having a big enough role to make real decisions, and still being hands-on enough to build things myself.

---

## Step 3 — Draft Your Career Vision Statement

Using your foundation questions as raw material, write a first draft of your Career Vision Statement. Aim for 3–5 sentences. Use the structure below as a guide — but feel free to depart from it once you have a draft:

> "My career vision is to [type of work] for/with [who you serve], so that [impact or outcome you want to create]. I want to be known as a professional who [qualities/values]. I see myself building a career in [field or sector] where I can [specific contribution], grounded in my commitment to [core value]."

**My Draft Career Vision Statement:**
My career vision is to build AI tools and food products for small food and beverage producers, so that they can make better products and grow their businesses without needing a big company's resources. I want to be known as a professional who finishes what I start and explains things clearly. I see myself building a career at the intersection of food and technology, where I can create software that handles costing, formulation, and scale-up, grounded in my commitment to quality and ownership.

---

## Step 4 — Refine and Finalize

Read your draft aloud (or read it slowly in your head). Ask yourself:

- Does this sound like me, or does it sound like what I think I should say?
- Is it specific enough to be meaningful?
- Is it broad enough to allow for growth?
- Does it excite me when I read it?

Revise as needed and write your final version below.

**My Final Career Vision Statement:**

> My career vision is to build technology and food products with small food and beverage producers, so that great products made by small teams can reach customers without needing a big company's resources. I want to be known as a professional who understands both the science and the software, finishes what I start, and explains hard things clearly. I see myself building a career at the intersection of food and technology, as a founder or a technical leader, where I can create tools that handle the costing, formulation, and scaling that small producers struggle with, grounded in my commitment to quality, ownership, and learning by doing.

**What I changed from my draft:** I changed "for" to "with" because I want to work alongside producers, not just hand them a tool. I added "founder or a technical leader" because my RIASEC results showed that I want ownership and decision-making, not only a technical job. And I left the field broad enough (food and technology) that it works whether I end up in a company, a startup, or my own business.

---

💡 **Note:** Your Career Vision Statement is a living document. You are not locked into it forever. You will revisit and revise it in Module 5 and beyond as your self-knowledge deepens and your career exploration continues.

## Reflection

**My Reflection:**
Writing this was easier than I expected because most of the pieces were already in my earlier work. What I hadn't done before was connect them in one sentence. The hardest part was staying specific. My first instinct was to write something broad like "use technology to help people," which sounds good but doesn't mean anything. Naming small food and beverage producers forced me to commit to a real group of people I can actually picture. I was surprised that nothing in the statement is about a job title. When I focused on the work and the people, the choice between AI developer, sales engineer, and food scientist mattered much less. This statement fits my plan: my Applied AI degree and Food Science minor give me both skills, my internship gives me experience, and my own brand and client work are already a start. I'll revisit it after my internship and in Module 5.
