### Career 1

**Career Title:**
Food Product Developer (O*NET lists "Product Development Scientist," "Formulator," "Food Technologist," and "Research Chef" as job titles under Food Scientists and Technologists)

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
A food product developer creates new foods and drinks and improves existing ones. Day to day, that means formulating and testing recipes at bench scale, adjusting ingredients to get the right flavor, color, texture, and shelf life, running sensory tests, and checking that raw ingredients and finished products are safe. They also write product specifications, make sure products meet FDA and USDA regulations, and work with suppliers, manufacturing, and marketing to move a product from concept to a production line.

**Typical Education Required:**
A bachelor's degree in food science or a related field such as chemistry, biology, or nutrition. According to the BLS, some employers prefer or require a master's or Ph.D., especially for research-heavy roles.

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Science: applying chemistry and microbiology to real ingredients
2. Critical thinking
3. Complex problem solving, such as fixing a formula that separates or goes stale
4. Quality control analysis
5. Data analysis, using analytical and data software to interpret test results
6. Communication: explaining results to scientists, producers, and consumers
7. Attention to detail

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Food production, production and processing, chemistry, mathematics, and English language (O*NET's top five). Food safety regulations and sensory evaluation are also central to the job.

**Median Annual Salary:**
$88,720 for food scientists and technologists (BLS, May 2025). The broader agricultural and food scientists group is $81,970.

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing. The BLS projects 6% employment growth from 2025 to 2035, which is faster than average, with about 2,700 openings per year across agricultural and food scientists.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
Most people start as a food technologist, R&D technician, or quality assurance technician, often after an internship. The BLS calls internships "highly recommended" because they often turn into full-time jobs. With experience, they move up to product developer, then senior scientist or R&D manager. A master's degree helps for research positions, and certifications such as IFT's Certified Food Scientist are optional but can help with advancement.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1. FDSC 410 – Food Chemistry, to understand why ingredients react and how flavor and texture hold up over time
2. FDSC 421 – Food Microbiology, for food safety, spoilage, and shelf life
3. Formulating and scaling products for my own functional confectionery and beverage brand, which is real product development with real costs

### Career 2

**Career Title:**
AI Applications Developer. The BLS and O*NET don't track "AI developer" separately, so they group it under Software Developers (O*NET 15-1252.00).

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
An AI applications developer builds software that uses AI to solve a specific problem for users, such as a chatbot, an automation tool, or a data dashboard. Day to day, they meet with users or clients to understand what they need, decide whether it can be built within the time and budget, write and test code, connect AI models and databases, fix bugs, and update the tool as needs change. They also document their work and report progress to clients or their team.

**Typical Education Required:**
A bachelor's degree in computer and information technology or a related field. Some employers prefer a master's for specialized roles, but a strong portfolio of real projects carries a lot of weight.

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Programming
2. Critical thinking
3. Complex problem solving
4. Systems analysis and evaluation: figuring out how the pieces of a system should work together
5. Judgment and decision making
6. Writing, for documentation and explaining work to clients
7. Creativity in design, with an eye on the user experience

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Computers and electronics, mathematics, customer and personal service, and English language (O*NET). For AI-specific work, add natural language processing, data handling, and AI ethics.

**Median Annual Salary:**
$135,980 for software developers (BLS, May 2025)

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing quickly. The BLS projects 10% growth from 2025 to 2035, which is much faster than average, with about 106,100 openings per year.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
People usually start as junior or associate developers after earning a degree and doing internships. Many also enter through freelance work or a portfolio of projects they built themselves. From there they advance to developer, senior developer, and then software architect or technical lead. According to the BLS, some move into project management or become computer and information systems managers. Some developers start their own companies or consultancies instead.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1. AI 301 – Natural Language Processing and Conversational AI, for building chatbots and language-based tools
2. INSC 384 – Database Design, since almost every real tool needs to store and organize data
3. Building websites and AI tools for my clients, plus my CECS 375 internship
