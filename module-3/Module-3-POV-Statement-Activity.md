# POV Statement Activity

Your POV Statement is the foundation of your entire Define Phase. It crystallizes your self-knowledge into a single, honest, actionable articulation of your academic direction. This is not a formal mission statement — it is a working tool. It will evolve. But you need a starting point, and this activity helps you build one.

---

## Step 1 — Review Your Module 2 Work (10–15 minutes)

Open your previous assignments and read through your key insights from:

- Your SWOT Matrix (especially Strengths and Opportunities)
- Empathy Map

As you read, highlight or note the three to five themes that appear most consistently. You are looking for patterns — things that showed up in multiple places.

**Themes I noticed:**
1. I want to build real things for real people, and my time goes into client projects and my own brand.
2. Technology and science are one interest for me, not two. Food science and AI keep showing up together.
3. I'm self-driven and entrepreneurial. I start things on my own and I want to own the results.
4. I learn fast by doing. I switched majors as a senior and I'm still on track.
5. I take on too much, and my biggest challenge is deciding what to leave out.

---

## Step 2 — Answer the POV Builder Questions

Write honest answers to each of the following questions. Do not overthink — write your first genuine response.

**1. Who am I in this moment, academically and personally?**
*(Consider: your background, your identity, your situation as a college student)*
I'm a senior at UT Knoxville in Applied Artificial Intelligence with a Food Science minor. I started in the College of Agriculture studying food science and switched into AI in Spring 2026, so my path hasn't been a straight line. Outside of class I'm building my own functional confectionery and beverage brand and making websites and AI tools for clients.

**2. What do I genuinely need from my college education?**
*(Consider: what would make this experience feel meaningful — not just successful)*
I need to finish with skills I can actually use, not just a diploma. That means real experience through an internship and projects I can show people, and a degree that lets me combine technology, science, and business instead of picking only one.

**3. What is the most important thing I learned about myself in Module 2?**
*(Consider: something surprising, clarifying, or that shifted how you see yourself)*
The most important thing was how clearly my strengths and weaknesses come from the same habit: I'm good at starting and building on my own, but I take on too much and don't have much experience working inside a company. I also learned that food science and AI aren't competing paths. They're one combined strength.

**4. What kind of work energizes me? What drains me?**
*(Consider: from your Empathy Map and Ikigai reflection)*
Building things energizes me, especially when I'm making a product or a tool and getting it in front of real users. Planning a launch or fixing something that doesn't work yet is when I lose track of time. Work that feels disconnected from a customer, or that is only paperwork and has no result I can see, drains me. So does juggling too many commitments at the same time.

**5. What problem or challenge do I most want to contribute to solving in the world?**
*(Consider: even a vague answer is useful here)*
I want to help small food and beverage producers. Making a product is only part of the work, and costing, sourcing, scaling, and explaining it clearly are underserved by the tools that exist. I'd like to build software that makes those parts easier for small producers.

---

## Step 3 — Draft Your POV Statement

Use the following template to draft your POV Statement. Write it in your own voice — not in formal or academic language. Write **at least two drafts** of your POV Statement and choose the one that feels most honest and most useful.

> "I am [brief description of who you are — background, identity, situation]. I need [a genuine academic or career need you identified]. Because [the most important insight from your self-assessment work]."

**Example:**
> "I am a first-generation college student from a small town who has always been drawn to science but also deeply values connection to people and community. I need a degree path that lets me combine analytical thinking with direct human impact, because I have learned that I lose motivation and meaning when my work feels disconnected from real people's lives."

### Draft 1
> I am a senior who started in food science and switched into Applied AI, and I'm building my own food and beverage brand on the side. I need a degree that gives me real technical skills and professional experience, because I've learned that I'm most motivated when I'm building something real.

### Draft 2
> I am a student who builds websites and AI tools for clients and develops food products for my own brand, and I don't want to choose between technology and food. I need a path that lets me combine science, technology, and business to make products people love, because I learned that my strengths and my energy come from taking an idea all the way to a customer.

### Final POV Statement
*(Choose the draft — or a refined combination — that feels most honest and most useful.)*

> I am a senior who started in food science, switched into Applied AI, and is building my own food and beverage brand and client business while finishing my degree. I need a path that lets me combine technology, science, and business so I can build products that people love, because I've learned that I'm most motivated and most capable when I take an idea all the way from the lab or the laptop to a real customer.

---

## Step 4 — Reflect

Below your final POV Statement, write a short paragraph (5–8 sentences) responding to the following:

- What was difficult about writing your POV Statement?
- What did you discover or clarify in the process?
- Does your POV Statement surprise you in any way?
- How does it connect to your Module 2 findings?

**My Reflection:**
The hardest part was fitting a path that has changed direction into one honest sentence, because it would have been easy to describe myself as only a food science student or only an AI student. Writing it helped me see that the thread running through all of it is building real products for people. It didn't surprise me that business showed up in the statement, but I hadn't expected to put it on equal footing with technology and science. It connects directly to my Module 2 work: my SWOT showed that my strengths come from making and building, and my empathy map showed that I'm happiest when my work reaches a real customer. The statement also reminds me of the weakness I found there, which is that I take on too much. I'll treat this as a working draft and revisit it after my internship.
