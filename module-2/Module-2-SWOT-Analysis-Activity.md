# Module 2: Personal SWOT Analysis

A SWOT analysis looks at where I stand right now: what I do well, where I'm weak, what chances are in front of me, and what could get in my way. I wrote this as a senior in Applied Artificial Intelligence with a Food Science minor, while building my own food and beverage brand and doing client work on the side.

---

## My SWOT Matrix

| | **Helpful** | **Harmful** |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal (about me)** | **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
| **External (around me)** | **Opportunities** | **Threats** |

### Strengths *(internal, helpful)*

1. **I know how to make and test a product.** I do product formulation for my own functional confectionery and beverage brand, so I understand how ingredients behave, not just recipes.
2. **I can build with technology.** I build websites and AI tools for clients, and I'm finishing a degree in Applied AI.
3. **I'm self-driven and I finish things.** Nobody assigned me my business or my client work. I started them and keep them running.
4. **I adapt quickly.** I switched from food science into Applied AI as a senior and I'm still holding a 3.64 GPA.
5. **My general education is done.** Every Volunteer Core requirement is complete, so all of my remaining time goes to my major and minor.

### Weaknesses *(internal, harmful)*

1. **I'm short on time.** Because I switched majors late, I have to fit most of my major into three semesters and a summer.
2. **I have no professional internship yet.** My experience is real, but it's all self-directed, so employers haven't seen me work inside a company.
3. **I take on too much at once.** Classes, my brand, and client projects compete for the same hours, and I tend to say yes to more than I can handle.
4. **The business side of my brand is still being learned.** Costing, sourcing, and scaling are harder than the formulation itself, and I'm still figuring them out.
5. **I have gaps in my science background.** My Food Science minor needs CHEM 260 and microbiology before I can take the last two minor courses.

### Opportunities *(external, helpful)*

1. **The CECS 375 internship** in Summer 2027 can give me professional experience and a reference.
2. **A gap in the market.** Small food producers have few good tools for costing, formulation, and scale-up, and that's exactly where my two backgrounds overlap.
3. **Strong demand for AI and software skills.** The BLS projects software developer jobs to grow 10% from 2025 to 2035.
4. **My Food Science minor makes me stand out.** Most AI graduates don't have food and beverage science knowledge.
5. **The CECS 399 and CECS 499 capstones** let me build a real project to show employers.

### Threats *(external, harmful)*

1. **Graduation risk.** If courses conflict or don't fit, my Fall 2027 graduation date could slip.
2. **Competition for internships.** Many AI and tech students are applying for the same summer positions.
3. **Fast-changing tools.** AI tools change quickly, so what I learn now can be outdated in a year if I stop practicing.
4. **Burnout.** Running a business and a full course load for another year is tiring.
5. **Cash and time limits in my brand.** Ingredients, packaging, and equipment cost money, and I'm funding it myself.

---

## What the Matrix Tells Me

**Strengths I should lean on:** being able to both make things and build things. Most people have one or the other.

**Weakness I most need to fix:** my lack of an internship, since it's what separates me from other applicants. I plan to apply to at least 10 internships by January 31, 2027.

**Opportunity I should act on first:** the CECS 375 internship, because it builds experience, a professional reference, and a stronger resume all at once.

**Threat I should watch most closely:** graduation timing. I need to meet with my advisor early so that my Food Science minor courses don't put Fall 2027 at risk.

---

## Reflection

Writing my SWOT showed me that most of my strengths come from doing things on my own, and most of my weaknesses come from the same habit. I'm good at starting and building, but I have little experience working inside an organization and I overload myself. The most useful insight is that my two backgrounds are not competing paths. Food science and AI are one combined strength, and the market gap I found is built around exactly that. The weakness that surprised me most was the business side of my brand, because I assumed formulation would be the hard part. Going forward, I'm going to treat my time as the scarce resource and be more selective about what I take on.
