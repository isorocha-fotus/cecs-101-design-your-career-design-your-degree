# Module 2: Personal Empathy Map

An empathy map looks at a person from the outside: what they think, feel, say, and do. For this assignment, I'm the person being studied. I tried to write what I observe about myself, not what I wish were true.

---

## My Empathy Map

### THINK — Beliefs and Inner Narrative

- "I'm behind, because I switched majors as a senior and now have to catch up fast."
- "If I keep building real things, the degree and the opportunities will follow."
- "I'd rather be making something people can use than writing a paper about it."
- "I don't have to choose between food and tech. I can do both."
- Sometimes: "I'm trying to do too much and something is going to slip."

### FEEL — Emotional Landscape

- **Excited** when a client project goes live or a product formula finally works.
- **Motivated** by the idea of building a real company, not just completing assignments.
- **Anxious** about the timeline: three semesters, a summer, and a full list of required courses.
- **Uncertain** about whether I should go all in on my own company or take a job after graduation.
- **Tired** when school, client work, and my brand all demand attention in the same week.

### SAY — Public Voice

- To family and friends: "I'm finishing my degree in Applied AI and building my own food and beverage brand."
- To clients: "I can build you a website or an AI tool that does what you need."
- To advisors and professors: "I switched into Applied AI and I'm planning to graduate in Fall 2027."
- What I usually leave out: how stretched my schedule actually is, and that I'm still unsure which direction I'll commit to after graduation.

### DO — Actual Behaviors

- I spend a lot of time building: websites, AI tools, and product formulas.
- I take on client work on top of a full course load.
- I plan things out carefully (my degree audit, semester schedule, and costs) instead of leaving them to chance.
- I tend to start new projects before I've finished the last one.
- I research tools and ideas on my own time without being asked to.

---

## Patterns and Gaps

**Where what I say and what I do match:** I say I want to build real products, and my time goes into exactly that: client projects and my own brand.

**Where there are gaps:**
1. **Say vs. feel.** I tell people my plan is clear, but privately I'm unsure whether I'll commit to my own company or a job.
2. **Think vs. do.** I believe I'm behind and need to focus, yet I keep adding new projects.
3. **Feel vs. do.** I feel tired when everything piles up, but I haven't built in a way to say no or slow down.

**Pains:** a tight timeline, too many commitments, and no professional experience yet.

**Gains:** a degree that combines AI and food science, a business I own, and a portfolio of real work.

---

## Reflection

The most useful part of the empathy map was seeing the gap between my thoughts and my behavior. I keep telling myself I'm behind and need to focus, but I still start new things. Once I saw that in writing, it was clear that my biggest challenge isn't ability, it's choosing what to leave out. I was also surprised to notice how often I leave out my uncertainty when I talk to other people. That tells me I should talk about the "company or job" question with my advisor and with people working in jobs I'm considering, instead of figuring it out alone. The things I do, building and finishing real projects, point clearly toward the kind of work I should look for.
